What is the assurance gap?

Autonomous platforms can progress from prototype to operational use quickly. Assurance means the evidence, testing, safety arguments, governance, and independent review used to show a system works within known limits. The gap is a practical pacing challenge: technology, operations, training, standards, and public evidence do not always mature at the same speed.1

Definitions

Autonomous system: a machine that performs part of a mission without continuous direct human control.

AI-enabled: using AI or machine learning to support sensing, planning, classification, or decisions.

T&E: test and evaluation—the process of checking whether a system performs safely and as intended.

ARL/EAR: Assurance Readiness Level and Export Assurance Readiness. ARL describes verification maturity; EAR describes readiness for export or co-development.

Tracker + Rubric

Assurance Readiness Rubric

A1 — Concept: Initial design or prototype; no structured safety case.
A2 — Early testing: Component tests and initial safety arguments.
A3 — Structured T&E: Formal test plans and early human-machine teaming trials.
A4 — Representative testing: Realistic testing, performance boundaries, and update controls.
A5 — Assurance-mature: Independent acceptance, lifecycle monitoring, controlled updates, and export-ready evidence.

Assurance debt: High at A1–A2, Medium at A3, Low at A4–A5.2

Export Assurance Readiness

Score 1–5 across public T&E evidence, independent validation, partner-standard alignment, governance transparency, and export precedent. 1.0–2.4: Emerging; 2.5–3.9: Developing; 4.0–5.0: Mature.3

Brief: A constructive assurance agenda

Executive summary

Australia is accelerating autonomous and AI-enabled defence capabilities. Testing, evidence, governance, training, and independent review must keep pace.9

For NOVA Systems

NOVA’s T&E Centre of Excellence and advanced practitioner training provide a platform for practical autonomy assurance.10

For BAE and Hunter

Hunter is a long-life naval program, making early assurance planning useful for future autonomous and uncrewed integration.11

For AI-autonomy firms

Assurance can support integration, allied collaboration, customer confidence, and export review by showing what has and has not been tested.12

Recommendations

  1. Use a common ARL/EAR vocabulary.
  2. Separate public summaries from protected evidence.
  3. Test human-machine handovers and failure modes early.
  4. Control AI updates before systems enter service.

Why it matters

For the public

Defence systems are publicly funded and influence safety, accountability, and Australia’s international relationships. Clear assurance explains how decision-makers manage risk and responsibility.13

This project is an attempt to clarify an important issue: capability can move quickly, but trust depends on the quality of the evidence, testing, accountability, and communication around it.

For sovereign capability and AUKUS

AUKUS Pillar II includes AI, autonomy, and undersea systems. Shared confidence in test, evaluation, security, and export-control processes supports meaningful collaboration.14

For ethics and law

Responsible AI in defence requires lawful use, human responsibility, appropriate controls, and evidence that systems operate within defined limits.15
