About this project

Why this project
matters.

AUKUS is reshaping how Australia, the UK and the US trade defence technology — but the rules only help the suppliers who can actually read them. This is a small, serious attempt to make that system more navigable for the people it affects most.

For the public and stakeholders

For Australian defence suppliers — especially small and mid-sized firms without in-house compliance teams — a misread of the Excluded Technologies List means lost revenue or, worse, a compliance breach. For prime contractors, cleaner supplier onboarding means faster programmes. For government and regulators, a more literate supplier base strengthens the sovereign capability AUKUS is meant to deliver. And for the public, plain-English transparency about what can and can’t be freely traded is a small but real guardrail on how defence dollars and technology flow.

Why this matters to me

This project sits at the intersection of policy, technology and national security. It started with a documented, verifiable gap in how export controls are understood across the Australian defence sector, and grew into an attempt to close it — reading the primary sources directly, working through how two regulatory systems interact, and building something that reflects that reading rather than a general impression of it. It is a genuinely self-directed piece of work. If it saves a supplier real time, or helps someone ask a sharper question of their compliance contact, it has done what it set out to do.
