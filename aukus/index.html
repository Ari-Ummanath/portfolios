The mission

Why this exists.

AUKUS is a trilateral agreement between Australia, the United Kingdom and the United States to cooperate more closely on defence technology. In September 2024, that cooperation took a significant step forward: most defence trade between the three countries no longer requires a special export licence. This is a genuine structural reform — it reduces friction across the alliance and helps capability reach the people who need it, faster.

The reform is not universal, however. A list of especially sensitive technologies — the Excluded Technologies List (ETL) — remains tightly controlled, and determining whether a given item falls within it takes specialist knowledge. Large prime contractors — the head contractors that hold the major government contracts and manage the supply chain beneath them — generally have dedicated compliance teams for this. Small and mid-sized Australian suppliers, who make up a significant share of the country’s defence industrial base, generally do not. A misjudgement in either direction carries real consequences: a company may decline business it was in fact entitled to pursue, or inadvertently breach the law. This tool exists specifically to help Australian suppliers get that judgement right, even though the underlying reforms apply across all three AUKUS partners.

For the general public

Defence trade rules aren’t just paperwork — they’re one of the guardrails on how sensitive technology and taxpayer-funded defence dollars move between countries. A more literate supplier base means fewer accidental breaches and a stronger, more trustworthy AUKUS partnership overall.

For suppliers & industry

If you run or work at a small or mid-sized Australian defence supplier, this tool turns a vague worry ("can I even ship this?") into a structured answer, with the sources to back it up and a clear list of what to check next.

For primes, government & policymakers

Every supplier who misreads the rules is friction in the supply chain. A more export-control-literate ecosystem means faster, cleaner onboarding for primes and a stronger case that AUKUS Pillar II can actually deliver at speed.

The gap that remains.

The reforms are genuine. Australia’s Defence Trade Controls Amendment Act 2024 and the US ITAR §126.7 exemption § = “Section” (interim final rule 20 Aug 2024; final rule 30 Dec 2025) removed most permit requirements for transfers among AUKUS partners. The Bureau of Industry and Security estimates about 80% of the value of existing US–Australia commercial defence trade is eligible for the exemption.

But the United States Studies Centre finds the remaining ~30% — the Excluded Technologies List — is the hard part: “small and medium-sized companies who lack the in-house expertise or financial capital to manage these compliance requirements are disincentivised from working with the US defence ecosystem.” That is exactly the tier feeding Australia’s prime contractors and the wider defence supply chain.
