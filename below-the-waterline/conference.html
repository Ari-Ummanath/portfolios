The ledger

The UN's own scientists now call it “water bankruptcy”

In January 2026, UN University's water institute argued that many regions have overspent their renewable water “income” and drawn down their long-term “savings” in aquifers, glaciers and wetlands.

75%of humanity lives in countries classed as water-insecure or critically water-insecure
4 bnpeople face severe water scarcity at least one month a year
70%of major aquifers show long-term decline
8×faster progress needed to reach universal safe drinking water by 2030; sanitation needs 6×
US$6.7 tnWorld Bank estimate of the water infrastructure gap by 2030

Why the 2023 baseline matters

Pledges without numbers cannot be tracked

The 2023 conference closed with about 700 voluntary commitments. Only 22% of the 719 that WRI analysed had quantitative targets. The 2026 concept papers lean the other way, proposing measurable targets such as 100 parties to the UN water conventions by 2030 and one private dollar for every public dollar.
