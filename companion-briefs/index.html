Executive summary

Global geopolitical-risk measures capture system-wide shocks, but institutional decision-making also requires standing regional interpretation. This briefing scores four Indo-Pacific and South Asian flashpoints and translates them into portfolio, operational, supply-chain, and compliance implications.12

Design proposition: use this as a recurring regional companion module alongside global risk indices, rather than as a substitute for them.

Regional flashpoints & implications

1. India-China border tension

Reported reductions in deployments and renewed high-level diplomacy point to partial stabilisation, but disputed-border risk remains intertwined with deep trade and supply-chain dependence.34 The practical risk channel is less an immediate large-scale conflict scenario than episodic escalation, policy tightening, and disruption to inputs critical to Indian manufacturing.

  • Monitor: LAC incidents, border-talk outcomes, FDI-screening changes, import restrictions, and Chinese-input dependency in electronics, pharmaceuticals, and critical minerals.
  • Institutional relevance: supply-chain diligence, country-risk limits, corporate exposure screening, and scenario design.

2. AUKUS submarine-timeline risk

AUKUS depends on US and UK industrial capacity as well as Australian capability development. Public reporting and parliamentary scrutiny have identified delivery, build-rate, and cost pressure as material execution risks.56

  • Monitor: US Virginia-class production, UK submarine-program milestones, workforce capacity, and trilateral funding commitments.
  • Institutional relevance: defence-industrial exposure, long-duration public spending, and shifts in regional deterrence assumptions.

3. Taiwan Strait shipping-lane exposure

The Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS)—a bipartisan, nonprofit policy research organization based in Washington, D.C.—estimates that more than one-fifth of global maritime trade transits the Taiwan Strait, making disruption a high-impact event for semiconductors, shipping, insurance, energy, and manufacturing supply chains.7

  • Monitor: military exercises, navigation restrictions, air and maritime incidents, war-risk insurance pricing, and freight diversion.
  • Institutional relevance: semiconductor concentration, sector stress testing, inflation scenarios, and logistics exposure.

4. India's strategic autonomy & sanctions-compliance predictability

India's effort to preserve strategic autonomy while maintaining major relationships across competing blocs can produce a complex operating environment for institutions subject to multiple sanctions and compliance regimes.89

  • Monitor: US secondary-sanctions posture, energy and infrastructure linkages, waiver changes, payment mechanisms, and regulatory guidance.
  • Institutional relevance: KYC, transaction screening, reputational risk, and India-related exposure scenarios.

How it integrates with existing tools

The intended use is a repeatable regional overlay: a concise scorecard, a clearly defined indicator set, and direct transmission channels to portfolio, compliance, and client decisions. JPMorganChase established its Center for Geopolitics in 2025 to help clients navigate structural uncertainty, while broader geopolitical-risk tools seek to measure global risk conditions.12

Suggested cadence: refresh quarterly and immediately after a material event; retain prior scores and explain every score change so the project develops an auditable history.

Brief 02 · Operational response module

Geopolitical Event Response Protocol for Passive Products

A pre-defined process for responding to impaired market access and geopolitical shocks before, during, and after index-provider action.

In this protocolProblem statementRussia 2022 caseEarly warningDecision frameworkEscalation protocolNotes & sources

Problem statement

Passive products track benchmarks under defined mandates, yet geopolitical shocks can rapidly impair trading, settlement, valuation, and investability. The 2022 Russia event demonstrated the time gap between an initial shock, fund-level dealing restrictions, and formal index-provider action.12

Protocol objective: do not attempt to override an index methodology. Instead, give asset managers a repeatable, governed method to prepare for impaired accessibility, communicate clearly, model consequences, and escalate evidence to index providers.

Worked case: Russia-Ukraine, February-March 2022

24 February 2022

Russia launches its full-scale invasion of Ukraine; sanctions and market-access risks accelerate.

28 February 2022

MSCI Inc. (formerly Morgan Stanley Capital International)—a global provider of equity, fixed income, and multi-asset indices—commences a consultation on the accessibility and investability of the Russian equity market.3

2–3 March 2022

Fund issuers take dealing and primary-market restrictions as market disruption and exchange closures intensify.45

7 March 2022

FTSE Russell removes Russian stocks from its equity indices, effective from the open on 7 March.6

9 March 2022

MSCI reclassifies Russia from emerging markets to standalone-market status, citing feedback on market uninvestability.3

Lesson: an institutional response framework should be activated when accessibility deteriorates—not only when an index decision has already occurred.

Early-warning indicators

Market accessibility

  • Exchange closures, persistent trading halts, capital controls, foreign-investor restrictions, or custodial/settlement failures.
  • Bid-ask dislocation, inability to create or redeem ETF shares, or inability to obtain reliable prices.

Geopolitical and legal triggers

  • Armed conflict, broad sanctions, credible blockade risk, emergency legislation, or designation of systemically important issuers.
  • Public consultation or formal review by an index provider concerning market classification or investability.

Institutional response signals

  • Custodian, exchange, broker, regulator, and index-provider notices are logged in a single evidence record.
  • Escalation is based on defined thresholds—not discretionary headline reaction.

Pre-index-provider action framework

Click each step to reveal details. The following sequence keeps legal, fiduciary, operations, and client interests connected.

1

Detect

Monitor trigger indicators and validate information.

Validate with exchange, regulator, and custodian notices. Log all evidence in a central record.
2

Assess

Convene risk, legal, compliance, portfolio, ops, and comms.

Cross-functional assessment to determine severity and required actions.
3

Stabilise

Restrict creations/redemptions if dealing is impaired.

Where permitted, suspend primary-market activity when reliable valuation is not possible.
4

Model

Calculate NAV, tracking-error, and liquidity scenarios.

Net Asset Value (NAV) is the per-share value of a fund's assets minus liabilities. Model outcomes under different index treatments (standalone market, full removal, zero pricing).
5

Communicate

Issue pre-approved client language on access limits.

Explain benchmark dependence, tracking-error risk, and next review points clearly.
6

Escalate

Provide evidence to index providers with standardised submission.

Seek a time-bound review using the escalation protocol below.
Governance guardrail: every action must remain subject to fund documents, applicable law, valuation policies, and fiduciary duties. This project is a research framework, not legal or investment advice.

Proposed faster escalation channel

  • Named contacts: establish identified escalation contacts at the asset manager and index provider.
  • Common evidence pack: use a short template for exchange notices, regulatory actions, settlement evidence, custody status, and observed pricing constraints.
  • Decision clock: agree a documented timetable for acknowledgement, preliminary assessment, and status update after a formal escalation.
  • Client-readiness pack: pre-approve FAQs, valuation explanations, and tracking-error disclosures before a crisis occurs.
  • After-action review: preserve a dated decision log and re-test thresholds after every material event.

This transforms crisis-time coordination from ad hoc communications into a more auditable operational process.

Project context

About the project

An Australia-based portfolio project demonstrating research, policy design, institutional communication, and geopolitical-risk thinking.

Purpose

To show how existing institutional geopolitical-risk tools can be extended through a tightly scoped regional brief and a practical passive-product response protocol.

Method

The project translates verifiable public reporting and institutional source material into defined indicators, decision-relevant implications, and clearly separated analytical judgements.

Live maintenance model

Refresh the flashpoint scores, dated source log, and "what changed" notes quarterly and following material geopolitical or market-access events.
