Case file 01 · open ICC ACU investigation

One over. One captain.
One federation, suspended.

On 17 February 2026, Canada's captain bowled a strange fifth over against New Zealand at the T20 World Cup. Five months later, Cricket Canada is suspended, its funding is frozen, and a documentary alleging a designated terrorist organisation reached its captaincy sits at the centre of a live ICC Anti-Corruption Unit case.

Case timeline

This timeline is built entirely from public reporting, laid out date by date. It's an unusual case: the investigation started because of a TV documentary, not a betting-monitor alert; the allegation is about who got picked as captain, not just one suspicious passage of play; and Cricket Canada has already been suspended and defunded — even though nobody has been charged with anything yet, five months on.

30 Sep 2025

Canada designates the Bishnoi network a terrorist entity — the 88th such listing, citing extortion and targeted killings against diaspora communities, including the murder of Punjabi singer Sidhu Moosewala. Tribune India ↗

Jan 2026

Dilpreet Bajwa named Canada's captain — the first turbaned Sikh to hold the role; a Punjab-born all-rounder who emigrated to Canada in 2020 after failing to make his home-state side. ESPNcricinfo ↗

17 Feb 2026

Canada v New Zealand, Chennai. In plain terms: Canada batted first and scored 173 runs while losing 4 wickets (“173/4”); New Zealand then chased that total down with two wickets to spare, finishing in 15.1 of their 20 overs (an “over” is six balls bowled). The flagged moment came in the 5th over of New Zealand's innings, with New Zealand at 35 runs for the loss of 2 wickets. Bajwa — normally a batter, not a regular bowler — unexpectedly came on to bowl, and delivered a “no-ball” (an illegal delivery that gives the batting side a free run and a retry) and a “wide” (a delivery too far from the batter to hit, also a free run) well down the leg side, conceding 15 runs in that one over. Al Jazeera ↗

10 Apr 2026

Cricket Canada announces new leadership — Arvinder Khosa as president, Paramjit Shahi as secretary, framed around “integrity, accountability and continued growth” — a week before the scandal breaks publicly. Cricket Canada ↗

17 Apr 2026

CBC's The Fifth Estate airs Corruption, Crime and Cricket. The report alleges Bishnoi-network infiltration facilitated Bajwa's rise to captain, and airs a recorded call in which former coach Khurram Chohan says board members pressured him on selection. Andrew Ephgrave, interim ACU general manager, confirms the ACU is “aware of the programme.” CBC ↗

~May 2026

1 Jun 2026

ICC suspends Cricket Canada's membership outright for “serious breaches of its membership obligations.” Players stay eligible for ICC events; remaining funds are gated through an ICC-controlled mechanism; a Normalisation Committee is installed. Global News ↗

19 Jul 2026

Cricket Canada reports “advancing rapidly” against confidential reinstatement conditions; no public timeline. Cricket Canada ↗

18 Sep 2026

Today. Cricket Canada remains suspended. The ACU investigation is open. No individual has been charged.

Exhibit B

How anomaly detection feeds ACU casework — and where this case breaks the pattern

The ICC Anti-Corruption Unit (ACU) runs a four-part model — prevention, disruption, investigation, prosecution — out of a central hub in Dubai. Andrew Ephgrave (a former British police officer) is interim general manager; Sumathi Dharmawardena PC, ex-Sri Lankan Attorney General's Department with Interpol and UNODC experience, chairs it. The allegation that first put this case on the ACU's radar came from a Canadian investigative documentary — CBC's The Fifth Estate ↗ — not from the ACU's own monitoring.

Ephgrave himself said the ACU was “aware of the programme broadcast by CBC” — in other words, journalism triggered this case, not a betting-market alert. That matters because Associate nations like Canada play far fewer heavily bookmaked, internationally broadcast matches than the ICC's Full Members. Fewer big bookmaked matches likely means thinner betting-market data in exactly the fixtures where governance is often weakest too — a gap that anomaly detection built around betting markets doesn't obviously fill.

Exhibit C · original argument

The diaspora angle: an early-warning asset, not only a vulnerability

The same diaspora channels the Bishnoi network is alleged to have used against Cricket Canada, it uses more broadly. Canadian intelligence reporting describes the network exploiting the student-visa stream ↗ to build a Canadian base; the gang itself has claimed over 1,000 “foot soldiers” in the country ↗. Its Canadian operations run on extortion aimed at the Punjabi diaspora — including a 2025 shooting at a Punjabi singer's Ontario home, claimed by the gang ↗, and the Moosewala assassination that led to the terrorist designation ↗. A cricket federation drawing its administrators and players overwhelmingly from that same community was a predictable target, not an unlikely one.

The current model

  • Centralised in Dubai, reliant on betting-market signal
  • No dedicated sensor inside diaspora community networks
  • What actually surfaced this case travelled through journalism, not a formal integrity channel
  • Community knowledge (a coach's pressured call, a captaincy that didn't track merit) reaches regulators late, if at all

What this case argues for

  • Structured, protected engagement with club committees, coaching/umpiring associations, diaspora sports media
  • A reporting line that sits with the ACU or an independent domestic-board integrity unit — never the federation's own board
  • Real anonymity and anti-retaliation protection, built with community organisations, not aimed at them
  • Turns the same social fabric organised crime exploits into the earliest possible sensor against it
Caveat

This runs straight into the risk it's meant to counter: Cricket Canada's own alleged compromise implicates its board, per the Chohan allegation — so any diaspora-reporting channel that feeds the federation's own leadership just becomes one more thing for a compromised board, or an infiltrating network, to capture. It has to sit outside that leadership entirely to work. Nobody in the current public-reporting or policy literature on ACU casework appears to be making this argument for cricket specifically, which is exactly why the Canada case is the clearest evidence for it so far.

Who's entangled, and what each needs

Six stakeholders, six different stakes.

Governance

Cricket Canada board & president

At stake: full ICC membership, 63% of revenue

Demonstrable separation between the board and team selection; public evidence of reform without breaching confidential reinstatement terms.

Operations

COO / operations

At stake: grassroots & domestic programme continuity

A funding plan independent of the frozen ICC channel; a duty-of-care communications line that protects Bajwa's presumption of innocence.

Regulator

ICC / Normalisation Committee / ACU

At stake: precedent for every diaspora-governed Associate

Verifiable evidence, not assurances; a process fast enough to protect the sport but fair enough to survive scrutiny with no charges yet filed.

Government

Public Safety Canada / Sport Canada

At stake: a designated terrorist entity reaching into a national sport body

Clarity on whether counter-transnational-repression tools extend to sport governance, coordinated with the ICC's own process.

Community

Public, media & the Punjabi-Canadian cricket community

At stake: an accurate story, not an ethnic-profiling one

Reporting that keeps the distinction explicit — the community is the target and the early-warning asset here, not the suspect.

Commercial

Sponsors & partners

At stake: brand exposure during an active suspension

Clear, dated updates on suspension status and the funding mechanism — not silence.
